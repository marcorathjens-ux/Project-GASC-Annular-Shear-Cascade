## 1. The Scaling Challenge (Volume Throughput)
While capillary forces (Xylem theory) are ideal for low-volume natural systems, industrial-scale GASC energy production requires high mass flow. To solve the volume constraint, the GASC-system operates as a **Binary Phase-Shift Cycle**.

## 2. Binary Architecture & Functional Principles
To ensure high-volume recirculation at low geothermal deltas (ΔT ≈ 20-30K), the system decouples falling mass from rising mass:

### A. Primary Loop (Open/Water): The Falling Mass
*   **Medium:** Water ($1000 kg/m^3$) is utilized in the fall shaft for maximum kinetic impact.
*   **Harvesting:** Adaptive Tesla Disc Turbines "shear" energy from the boundary layer (Annular Shear), converting gravitational potential into rotational energy.

### B. Secondary Loop (Closed/ORC): The "Thermal Lift"
*   **Medium:** A low-boiling Organic Rankine Cycle fluid (e.g., R245fa or Isobutane) circulates in a sealed heat-pipe array.
*   **Evaporation (Bottom):** Geothermal heat (approx. 35°C+) and accumulated waste heat from the Tesla turbines evaporate the ORC-medium. The vapor rises passively due to buoyancy and density differences.
*   **Condensation (Top):** Cooler surface air (approx. 15°C) acts as the "leaf" condenser. The medium reverts to liquid, maintaining a continuous pressure-sink that pulls the vapor upward.

## 3. Synergistic Efficiency
*   **Thermal Boosting:** GASC is not just a harvester but a thermal integrator. The friction heat generated during the shearing process (Q-waste) is directly recycled into the ORC-evaporator, increasing the lift velocity of the return flow.
*   **No Mechanical Pumping:** The return flow is entirely "financed" by the geothermal gradient between the mine depth and the surface.
